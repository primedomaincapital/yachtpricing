The price tape for yachting

Yacht prices are negotiated in private and announced, if at all, as asking prices — which makes the monthly brokerage record the closest thing the market has to a price index. Read across a year of those months and the pattern is clear: fewer trophy sales at the very top, steady volume in the middle, and a market that rewards newer, larger, well-maintained yachts while older tonnage waits.

Monthly averages swing wildly — €9.3 million one month, €42.8 million the next — because a single very large sale can double a month's total. That volatility is the point, not a flaw: it shows a thin market at the top where one transaction rewrites the average. Broader context from the same coverage puts 2025 brokerage sales at €7.5 billion, with total value holding firm even as transaction counts eased from pandemic highs, as buyers shifted toward larger, higher-specification vessels. Source: BOAT International figures, via Boating NZ

Methodology

Included: BOATPro/BOAT International monthly brokerage reports for yachts of 24 metres and above, the 2024 top-sales review, and a published market-size estimate. Excluded: yachts under 24 metres (no verified aggregate this session), individual asking prices presented as sale prices (only Phoenix 2's asking price is shown, labeled as such), and any IYBA aggregate — none was verified this session. Asking prices are not sale prices; where a total is labeled a sales value, it follows BOATPro's convention.
